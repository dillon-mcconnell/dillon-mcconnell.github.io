SpaceX's Turbulent First Month as a Public Company

July 2026 Professional
Morningstar Direct Excel Flourish

Overview

A month earlier I had used Morningstar holdings data to flag the mutual funds holding pre-IPO stakes in SpaceX. Then the company went public, and the debut turned out to be one of the wildest in recent memory. The stock priced at a $1.8 trillion market cap, ran up to a peak of nearly $2.7 trillion, and sat at $1.9 trillion as of the July 10 close. I pulled daily pricing and market cap data from Morningstar Direct to track those swings and built a set of charts covering the first month.

What the Data Showed

Roughly $800 billion in market cap was erased between the peak and the July 10 close. That was more than the entire value of household names like Visa, Exxon Mobil, or Coca-Cola. The sharpest stretch came between June 16 and June 22, when SpaceX shed more than $600 billion in under a week.

Even after all of that, SpaceX ranked as the 7th most valuable publicly traded company in the world, sitting just behind TSMC and ahead of Broadcom and Saudi Aramco.

I also wanted context for whether the drop was unusual, so I compared SpaceX's post-IPO returns against other heavily anticipated listings from the past decade over the same number of trading days. Measured that way it looked worse, not better. SpaceX was down almost 10% through 18 trading days, second worst in the group. Only Arm had a rougher start, while Robinhood and Rivian ran far hotter early on.

Why the Volatility

Morningstar equity analyst Nicolas Owens tied the swings to a gap between investor enthusiasm and the growth assumptions already priced into the shares:

"In our opinion, the offering implies that investors have already priced in flawless execution across multiple unproven frontiers (reusable Starship, orbital data centers) simultaneously. The offering price appears to bake in optimistic execution scenarios from the outset. As a result, even modest shortfalls or disappointments, whether in launch cadence, data center commercialization, Starlink growth, or regulatory approvals, should prompt corrections to the company's valuation. In our view, the IPO valuation offers limited upside for long-term oriented investors, but the risk of a downside surprise is significant."
